Communicable Diseases

Measles
(Rubeola)

"Highly contagious, but entirely preventable."


Agent & Transmission

The Agent

  • Pathogen: Paramyxovirus (RNA virus)
  • Also known as: Rubeola, "Hard measles", "Red measles"
  • Incubation Period: 10 to 14 days
  • Period of Communicability: 4 days before to 4 days after the appearance of the rash.

Transmission Dynamics

Measles is one of the most highly contagious infectious diseases known to man.

Airborne Transmission

Spread primarily via microscopic aerosolized droplets that remain active and contagious in the air or on infected surfaces for up to 2 hours.

Direct Contact

Also spread by direct contact with infected respiratory secretions.

Clinical Pearl: Measles vs. German Measles. Measles (Rubeola) is caused by the Paramyxovirus and is severe ("hard measles"). German Measles (Rubella) is caused by the Togavirus and is generally milder ("3-day measles"), but poses severe teratogenic risks to pregnant women.

Clinical Manifestations

The clinical course of measles is typically divided into distinct stages. Recognizing these early is crucial for isolation and management.

1. Pre-eruptive / Prodromal Stage

Lasts 3-4 days before the rash appears. The patient is highly contagious here.

The "3 C's" & High Fever

  • High fever (can spike >40°C / 104°F)
  • Cough (usually dry, hacking)
  • Coryza (runny nose)
  • Conjunctivitis (red, watery eyes; photophobia)

Pathognomonic Sign

Koplik Spots

Tiny bluish-white spots with red halos found on the buccal mucosa (inside the cheeks, opposite the molars). They appear 1-2 days before the rash and fade as the rash erupts.

2. Eruptive Stage (The Rash)

Characterized by a maculopapular rash (flat red areas covered with small confluent bumps).

  • Direction: Spreads Cephalocaudally (from head to toe).
  • Starts: Behind the ears, on the hairline, and upper neck.
  • Progression: Spreads downward to the trunk and extremities over 3 days.
  • Note: Fever often peaks simultaneously with the eruption of the rash.

3. Convalescent Stage

The rash begins to fade in the exact same order that it appeared (top to bottom). As it fades, it leaves a brownish discoloration (hyperpigmentation) and fine peeling of the skin (desquamation).

Measles Complications

Measles suppresses the immune system, leaving the body highly vulnerable to secondary bacterial and viral infections. Complications are most common in children <5 years and adults >20 years.

Most Common

Otitis Media

Middle ear infection is the most frequent complication of measles, which can lead to permanent hearing loss.

Most Fatal (Leading Cause of Death)

Pneumonia

Most measles-related deaths are due to respiratory complications (either primary viral pneumonia or secondary bacterial pneumonia).

Neurological

Encephalitis

Acute encephalitis can occur. A rare, fatal, late complication (years later) is SSPE (Subacute Sclerosing Panencephalitis).

Other complications: Corneal ulceration (leading to blindness, especially when compounded by Vitamin A deficiency), severe diarrhea, and croup.

Prevention & Nursing Care

Primary Prevention: Vaccination

The Vaccine

Measles vaccine is a Live Attenuated vaccine. It is usually given combined as MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella).

EPI Schedule (Philippines)

  • 1st dose: 9 months
  • 2nd dose: 12 - 15 months

Nursing Management

1. Strict Airborne Precautions

Isolate the patient immediately. Use an N95 mask, negative pressure room if available in hospital. Keep child away from other children at home for at least 4 days after rash onset.

2. Vitamin A Supplementation

WHO strictly recommends giving Vitamin A to all children diagnosed with measles. It significantly reduces morbidity, blindness, and mortality rates by supporting immune function and epithelial integrity.

3. Supportive / Symptomatic Care

  • Fever: Antipyretics (Paracetamol) and TSB. Avoid Aspirin in children to prevent Reye's syndrome.
  • Photophobia: Dim the lights in the room; encourage the child to wear sunglasses if needed.
  • Skin care: Keep skin clean and dry. Use mild, non-irritating soaps. Calamine lotion for itching.
  • Nutrition/Hydration: Soft, bland diet (buccal mucosa is inflamed from Koplik spots). Encourage oral fluids to prevent dehydration.
